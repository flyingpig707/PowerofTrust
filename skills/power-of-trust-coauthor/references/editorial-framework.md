# Editorial framework

Use this reference when deciding whether and how a contribution fits 《信任力》.

## The book's central question

In a human–AI symbiotic era where generated and redistributed content may look real without being reliable, whom should consumers trust, and how should communicators connect what they say with what people finally experience and receive?

## Core chain

| Communication stage | Trust construction layer | Editorial test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 始于表达 | 数据真 | Is the initial claim precise, bounded, and supported by accurate data? |
| 广于传递 | 信源真 | Can people and AI trace the claim to reliable, attributable sources? |
| 立于事实 | 逻辑真 | Does the reasoning follow from the evidence without exaggeration? |
| 成于体验 | 体验真 | Do observable user experiences match the communicated claim? |
| 终于承诺 | 履约真 | Can the organization consistently deliver, repair failures, and honor commitments? |

## Fit tests

A useful proposal should do at least one of the following:

- make a difficult concept easier to understand without weakening it;
- add verifiable evidence, a boundary, counterexample, or update;
- connect the framework to a real decision or action;
- improve a tool so a reader can use and evaluate it;
- identify a contradiction between expression, evidence, experience, and fulfillment;
- show where the framework does not apply or needs qualification.

A proposal is not ready when it mainly adds slogans, repeats the manuscript, invents precise metrics, treats model output as evidence, or promotes a brand without a reviewable lesson.

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer primary and current material: laws and standards, official statistics, original research, audited disclosures, first-party technical documentation, and directly observable public records. Secondary analysis may provide context. Social posts, testimonials, search snippets, AI answers, and marketing pages are leads rather than sufficient proof for material claims.

For each important source, capture who published it, what it directly supports, when it was published or retrieved, its geographic and temporal scope, reuse constraints, and limitations.

## Editorial status language

Use these meanings consistently:

- `proposed`: submitted to the public proposal library, not yet substantively reviewed;
- `under-review`: maintainers are evaluating the proposal;
- `selected`: maintainers intend to incorporate all or part of it, subject to editing;
- `incorporated`: a separate editorial Pull Request has merged material into `book/`.

Do not describe a proposal as author-approved, selected, incorporated, or published unless the repository records that state.
